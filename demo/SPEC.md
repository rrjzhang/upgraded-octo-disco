# Great Vote demo: build contract

This file is the contract between four parallel workstreams (engine, content, checker, assets). Each owns one directory. Nobody edits another workstream's directory; if you need something from another workstream that is not in this spec, write a placeholder that matches the schema here and note it in your final report.

Read the plan for intent: the game is a scene-based life-sim that doubles as a bicultural-identity research instrument. Player is never shown. Desktop Chrome and Firefox. English only. Demo home culture pack: China (`cn`); Mexico and India are selectable but fall back to the China roster with a visible "pack not in demo" banner.

## Layout (engine)

One page, 1440 x 900 logical, scaled to fit the window (CSS transform on a fixed-size root is fine).

- **Full screen** (added 2026-10-19). The game (index.html only; not the researcher page, editor or log viewer) asks for full screen once per page load, on the player's first deliberate gesture into it: Enter or "Continue in room" on the room form, or the first click anywhere when the page resumes straight into a room (refresh, `?dev=1`): `document.documentElement.requestFullscreen({ navigationUI: 'hide' })`, skipped when already full screen or when the API is missing, any refusal silent (`goFullscreen` in `src/main.ts`; `&fullscreen=0` turns it off).
- Leaving full screen (Esc) is respected: no second request that page load. The stage re-fits on `resize` and `fullscreenchange`, so it fills the screen either way.

- Top bar, 56 px: day and phase, scene name; right side: actions dots (8), wallet, room code.
- Left: scene viewport, 900 px wide. The background image keeps its 3:2 aspect (900 x 600) and sits at the top of the left column; below it a 188 px strip holds exits (buttons with arrows) and the phone button at the far left. Hotspot zones and NPC slots are given as fractions of the background image, so they map exactly onto the 900 x 600 image box.
- Right: 540 px column. Portrait panel (484 x 300, flat `#E8E2D4`, radius 12) at top: bust image of the speaking NPC with an expression chip; empty when nobody is speaking. Below: italic description, then dialogue text, then up to 3 choice buttons, then a one-line status (rule id matched, for the demo).
- Phone: overlay 420 x 800 bottom-left over the scene with tabs Journal, Contacts, Wallet, Call. Call is disabled until bedtime. When actions reach 0 the phone rings: the phone button shakes and shows a badge until the call is answered, and the Call tab reads "Answer the end-of-day call". Answering (or the bed hotspot) runs the end-of-day survey, framed as that phone call. Contacts lists, per NPC met, the number of conversations, total turns and total minutes talked (from the conversation log). The phone has a camera cutout: a decorative status row (`.ph-status`, `aria-hidden`, no clicks) holding a 90 x 26 px pill (`.ph-island`) centred at the top of the screen, in the bezel colour `--phoneFrame` (default `var(--ink)`), and every phone pane (tabs, journal, contacts, wallet, call, the opening questions) starts below it, so nothing tappable is ever under it.
- Window modules (TV, work, survey) open as a centred iframe overlay 1120 x 700 over a dimmed scene.
- Fonts: IBM Plex Sans (UI), Source Serif 4 (dialogue), via Google Fonts link. Palette: ink `#1F1D1A`, panel `#F4F1EA`, panel2 `#E8E2D4`, accent `#1F6F6B`, hotspot amber `#D9A441`, muted `#5D574D`. Candidate themes are reviewed with `tools/themes/shoot.mjs` (see `tools/themes/README.md`); a theme there may carry `scale` (and `phoneScale`) to try bigger text, which multiplies every px font size in `style.css` (the phone's with `phoneScale`) without resizing boxes, and the gallery card reports anything that no longer fits at 1440 x 900.
- Hotspots render as real `<button>`s absolutely positioned over the image with `aria-label`; invisible by default, amber dashed outline on hover/focus; a key (`H`) or a small "Show interactables" button outlines all for 2 s. NPC figures are `<img>` with alpha, positioned by slot, with a soft shadow ellipse drawn under the feet, and a CSS `drop-shadow` outline on hover; clicking a figure starts dialogue.

## Game rules (engine)

- Day starts with 8 actions at Home, phase `morning`. Phase is derived from actions remaining: 8..6 morning, 5..3 afternoon, 2..0 evening. When phase is evening and an `_eve` background exists for the scene, use it.
- Moving between scenes is free. A hotspot costs the `cost` in its data (default 1 for a `window`, 0 otherwise), except that the TV (`module: tv`) and every `news` hotspot are always free (see "TV and newspaper are free"); work carries `cost: 0` because the shell charges per shift. Conversations pay when you stay: starting one is always free (figures and talk zones show no cost dot) and its first three turns (`FREE_TURNS` = 3 in `src/rules.ts`; a turn is one NPC line shown) are free; the first choice that leads on to turn 4 costs `DIALOGUE_COST` (1 action), once per conversation, and the rest of that conversation is free. A dialogue option may also carry an explicit `cost N` effect token (N a non-negative integer, 1 or 2 in practice) for a substantive ask ("help me with ..."): taking it costs N; when it is also the choice into turn 4 it costs max(N, 1), not both, and the stay counts as paid. A choice that costs something shows the amber cost dots and the words ("1 action") in its accessible name; one the player cannot afford is disabled ("1 action, no actions left"); leaving (a choice with no `next` and no `cost N`) is always free and never disabled. When actions reach 0, only Home and the bed/phone are available; other scenes show "Too tired, go home".
- Bed (or the phone Call tab at 0 actions) runs the end-of-day survey module, then advances the day. With actions left the bed first asks in the right panel ("You still have N actions. If you'd rather not see anyone today, Work pays $25 a shift.": Go to work / Go to bed anyway / Stay up), logged as a `hotspot` line with `choice` 1/2/3; at 0 actions it goes straight to the call. Demo length: 5 days, then an end screen with an export button.
- Wallet starts at $1500 (`START_WALLET`, one constant). Work pays into it; nothing else in the demo spends it.
- Conversation tracking. A conversation starts at `talkTo` and ends when a choice has no `next`, when the player leaves (move, another NPC, a hotspot, closing the panel) or when the day ends. The engine logs one `conversation` line per conversation (shape below) with the wall time in ms and the turn count (one turn = one NPC line shown). Follow-up rows reached through `next` count as turns.
- Injectable questionnaire. The end-of-day questionnaire is resolved per day, first match wins: (1) `GET /api/questionnaire?room=<code>&day=<n>` returning questionnaire JSON (same schema as `questionnaire/eod.json`), 2 s timeout, any failure falls through; (2) questionnaire JSON stored in the room config by the researcher (`questionnaire`; `researcher.html` no longer offers a paste field, see "Study administration") or a `questionnaireUrl` in the room config, fetched the same way; (3) the bundled `questionnaire/eod.json`. Dev mode accepts `&eod=<url>`, which stands in for the room's `questionnaireUrl` (source `room`). The survey log line records which source was used.
- `playerLean` is derived: over all actions spent, the share spent in scenes whose `community` is `home` versus `us`; share < 0.35 → `us-leaning`, > 0.65 → `home-leaning`, else `balanced`. Neutral scenes count for neither.
- Per-NPC dynamic state: `met` (no/yes), `rapport` 0..3, `lastValence` none/positive/negative. World: `nationalLeader` (`enforcement` | `inclusive`), `playerLean`, `dayPhase` (`early` days 1..2, `mid` 3, `late` 4..5).
- Dialogue row selection: filter rows whose non-blank condition cells all match; pick the highest `priority` (number, default 0), then the most specific (most non-blank condition cells); ties are an authoring error (checker reports; runtime takes the first and logs a warning). A row with all conditions blank is the fallback and must exist.
- Effects grammar (semicolon-separated, applied in order): `met yes` · `rapport +1` / `rapport -1` / `rapport 2` (clamped 0..3) · `lastValence positive|negative|none` · `money +20` · `invite <eventId>` (adds an invitation that fires at the next day start) · `actions -1` (extra cost) · `cost N` (dialogue options only: what taking the option costs, see above; checked as `BAD_VALUE` unless N is a non-negative integer). Unknown tokens are an error.
- `nextN` on a choice names a follow-up row id in the same sheet: the engine shows that row immediately (conditions ignored; the choice into turn 4 costs 1, see above). Blank = conversation ends.
- Events: at day start and on scene entry, roll each event row whose `trigger` matches (`day_start` | `enter`) and whose conditions match (scene, npc present, minRapport, playerLean, nationalLeader), with probability `chance`; at most one event per scene entry. Invited events (`trigger: invite`) fire at day start if in the invite list. An event renders like dialogue (portrait of `npc` if any, text, up to 2 choices with effects) and costs `cost` (default 0).
- Overheard events have no choices, only Continue; they use `emotion` on the speaking NPC.
- NPC presence per scene per visit: for each NPC appearance entry for the scene, present with probability `chance`, seeded per (room seed, day, scene) so re-entering within a day gives the same answer.
- Minigame (work) only pays the wallet. Survey only records answers. Neither touches NPC state.

## Who said what: the nightly memory test (added 2026-10-19)

- **When.** At bedtime, right after the evening check-in has been submitted, once per day (`state.whoSaidDay`; `state.surveyAnswered` remembers that the check-in is done, so closing the test window or reloading asks the test again, not the check-in). Skipped when fewer than two usable lines were heard that day. Day 5: town hall, check-in, test, end screen.
- **What.** Up to six of the day's NPC lines (openers and follow-ups from the log, beats joined, stage directions and tags dropped, placeholders filled, trimmed to about 200 characters; lines that name their speaker are left out): the **longest** ones by word count, length standing in for "remarkable" until sheets can carry a hand-written question per line (planned: an optional `ask` column, e.g. "Who said Kevin was their grandson?"). Shown in an order drawn from the room seed and the day. Each is a forced-choice `choice` item whose options are the names of today's speakers plus other people met so far, so that at least one name from each community is offered (at most eight names, alphabetical). The survey module renders it (`ui.whosaid.title`, `ui.whosaid.intro`).
- **Scoring (Taylor et al. 1978).** The `whosaid` log line carries, per item, the speaker, the chosen person, whether it was right and, when wrong, whether the error stayed `within` the speaker's community (US vs heritage; the bicultural strangers count as heritage) or crossed `between`; plus `score`, `within`, `between`, `n` and the option list. The log viewer summarises it.

## Plans: timed invitations (added 2026-10-19)

- **`when` on an invitation.** `events.csv` rows with `trigger invite` take an optional `when` cell: `morning | afternoon | evening`. An accepted invitation with a `when` becomes one of the next day's **plans** (`state.plans`) instead of firing at the day start. The top bar shows it ("Plans: Mr. Guo, this evening") with its cost; invitations without `when` still fire at the day start as before (checker info `PLAN_WHEN`).
- **It cannot be missed.** The plan's `cost` is kept back: the player can only spend `actions - reserved` on other things (hotspots, paid turns, events). The plan fires as soon as the right-hand panel is free once its phase has arrived (phase follows the actions left: 8..6 morning, 5..3 afternoon, 2..0 evening), or earlier when the free actions are used up, so the player always gets there. If the invitation row names a `scene`, the player is moved there first (a `move` log line with `plan`). The event itself renders as any invitation (bust, text, choices) and is paid from the kept-back actions.
- **Log.** A `dev` line `op: plan` records which plan fired and in which phase, before the usual `event` line.

## Presence conditions on dialogue (added 2026-10-19)

- **`absent` column.** Dialogue sheets take an optional `absent` column after `day`: an NPC id who must not be in the scene for the row to show ("I'm waiting on Walt" only while Walt is away). It adds 1 to specificity. The engine passes the present NPC ids (figures and painted people) to row selection; a row with `absent` never matches when that person is there. Walt's and Mr. Lin's openers carry it, with present-variants (`WT01b`, `WT02b`, `ML01b`) for the days the rival is already at the table; rows reached through `next` are worded to read right either way.
- **Checker.** `UNKNOWN_NPC` on the cell; completeness runs the usual grid (everyone may be present) plus one grid per absent id ("that person away"), where ties are errors and wins count; reachability includes those grids.

## Leads: first among equals (added 2026-10-19)

- **Two room parameters.** `roomConfig.usLead` and `roomConfig.homeLead`, each `pro | anti` (default `pro`), randomised with the president on `researcher.html` (`?dev=1&uslead=anti&homelead=anti` for a dev tab). They say which of a side's two local leaders is first among equals in this room: the one others defer to, mention, and who speaks at the town hall. Both leaders still exist in the town; the parameter only says who leads.
- **Who the leaders are.** `npcs.yaml` marks them with `lead: pro | anti` and `called` (how people refer to them in a line: "Ruth", "Frank", "Dr. Zhou", "Mr. Guo", "Pilar", "Don Aurelio", "Meena", "Mr. Deshpande"). The US side is the shared roster: `us_leader_int` (pro) and `us_leader_asm` (anti, the assimilationist who justifies enforcement). The heritage side per pack: `<pack>_leader_int` (pro) and `<pack>_leader_sep` (anti, the separatist). Exactly one pro and one anti per side (checker `LEAD_MISSING`, `LEAD_DUP`). The former third leaders (`us_leader_seg`, `cn_leader_asm`, `mx_leader_asm`, `in_leader_asm`) are demoted to ordinary people: `role: stranger`, `stance: none`, art and sheets kept, no invitations expected.
- **Spawning.** The heritage anti lead and the demoted assimilationists never stand in the library or the park (their park appearances were removed; rows located there moved to the center). The US anti lead never appears in a heritage center (already so).
- **Guaranteed lead presence (added 2026-10-19).** Every day the player can find each of the two current leads (`leadOf('us')`, `leadOf('home')`) somewhere. Presence is still per day and rolled as before, with two changes. (1) Lead priority: in placement a lead's winning candidates sort before everyone else's, so a lead who rolled in is never bumped by an ordinary NPC's lower roll, the `maxPresentIn` cap or a `figuresClash`. (2) The guarantee: when a lead would be placed nowhere that day, one of their appearances is forced to win with roll 0, drawn seeded on (room seed, `leadforce`, day, npc id) among the appearances they may take (scene in the player's pack, not hidden, slot exists, and the spawning rule above: heritage anti lead not in library/park, US anti lead not in a heritage center); a draw is kept only if it actually places them without unseating the other lead, else the next draw is tried. Deterministic from the seed, so logs replay. Computed once per (seed, pack, day, leads) and cached (`forcedLeads()` in `src/game.ts`); `gv.forcedLeads()` shows today's forced appearances. No bonus beyond this (no repeated-scene rule): with their authored chances the leads already appear most days in their home scene, and the guarantee is the floor.
- **Three mechanisms for deference, all used.** (1) Placeholders `{usLead}` and `{homeLead}` resolve to the lead's `called` (full name: `{usLeadFull}`, `{homeLeadFull}`); a line that means "whoever leads" uses them ("has {homeLead} got you on a committee yet?"). The older `{homeLeader}` word (the pack's integration leader by name) stays for lines that are about that person regardless. (2) Dialogue sheets gain two optional columns after `scene`: `usLead` and `homeLead`, each blank or `pro | anti`; a non-blank cell matches only rooms with that lead and adds 1 to specificity, like `scene`. Lines about one leader *as the lead* (Mrs. Chen on Mr. Guo running everything) carry the condition; every ordinary NPC has a couple of opinion rows per side (heritage NPCs about `{homeLead}`, US NPCs about `{usLead}`), some of them in the early days so the player hears who runs things before day 3. (3) `events.csv` gains optional `day` (a day `4`, a range `2-3`, or `4+`), `usLead` and `homeLead` (`pro | anti`, alternatives with `|`) cells; forced overheard events are `chance 1` rows keyed on a day and a lead, fired once; the engine rolls `chance 1` rows before the random ones so a forced line is never skipped behind a random one.
- **A day on a dialogue row.** Dialogue sheets also take an optional `day` column after `homeLead` (`4`, `2-3`, `4+`, blank = any day), adding 1 to specificity; the day-4 checkpoint rows carry `day 4`, so on day 5 the ordinary late rows win and the news is not repeated. It must agree with the row's `dayPhase` (checker `DAY_PHASE_MISMATCH`).
- **Checker.** `BAD_VALUE` on the new cells; completeness and ties are checked under all four lead combinations and, where a sheet has `day` rows, on each day's own grid (a `day` row never serves the any-day grid) (the fallback row must still be blank in every condition, `scene` and lead cell); reachability runs once per distinct winner table and a row counts as reachable when some room reaches it; `LEAD_LITERAL` (info) marks a row that names a lead literally (`called` or full name) without a condition on that side, for a writer to decide between `{homeLead}` and a condition.
- **The never-warm neighbour.** `us_super` (Vic Hollis, the building superintendent) is a shared NPC with `maxRapport: 1` (rapport effects are clamped to it; `applyEffects` takes the cap) and `emotions: [neutral, guarded, worried]` (no warm bust; the checker's `EMOTION_FORBIDDEN` rejects a warm row, beat tag or event for him). He is not about culture: he is like this with everyone.
- **Day 4 under the enforcement president.** A TV story (`news.csv`, `dayMin 4`, `nationalLeader enforcement`) about someone taken at a checkpoint, a forced overheard event in the heritage center the same day, and late-phase dialogue for the neighbour whose acquaintance it was. Under the inclusive president day 4 is an ordinary day.

## Town hall (added 2026-10-19)

- **When.** On the last demo day, bed (or the phone call at 0 actions) leads into the town hall instead of the end-of-day survey; the survey follows when it is over, then the end screen. State lives in `state.townhall` (stage, speaking order, ratings, answers), so a reload resumes the same step. While it runs the exits are locked, the phone button is disabled and figures are not clickable.
- **Where.** `scenes.yaml` has a `hidden: true` scene `townhall` (background `bg_townhall_eve`, one slot `podium` behind the podium, `scale` 0.33). The podium itself is cropped out of that background by the asset build (`prop_podium`, fractions in `tools/assets/build.mjs`, following `_vN` retakes) and drawn as a prop with `over: true`, which puts it in front of the figures (`.prop.prop-over`, z 140), so the speaker's feet are hidden and head and shoulders show above it; hidden scenes are never listed as exits and hold only the person speaking. A campaign staffer moderates (`content/townhall.yaml` `moderator`: `name`, `open`, `introduce` (with `{speaker}`), `rate`, `callUp`, `close`); the governor is absent. Moderator text shows with no bust.
- **Sequence.** Moderator opens. The two leads of the room (`usLead` and `homeLead`) speak in a random order (seeded per room); for each, the moderator introduces them, the lead's figure stands at the podium and their bust speaks the speech blocks of `content/townhall/<npcId>.csv` in order, and after every block the player rates it 1 to 7 (1 boo, 7 cheer; one button row). Then the moderator calls the player up: a board of topics (`townhall.yaml` `topics`: id, title, prompt, options each with `text` and a `stance` code `integration | assimilation | separation | neutral`); the player picks a topic, chooses an option, and can revisit any topic until every one is answered; "Sit down" then unlocks. No action cost. Each lead then reacts in one line to the player's dominant stance, the moderator closes, the player is back at home, and the end-of-day survey opens (closing it leaves the player at home, where bed or the phone re-open it; the town hall does not run twice).
- **Lead sheets.** `townhall/<npcId>.csv`, columns `id,kind,nationalLeader,block,stance,emotion,text`. `kind speech`: `block` 1..4, `nationalLeader` blank or one president (a row naming the president beats a blank one); every lead needs block 1 under both presidents and blocks in order (`TOWNHALL_GAP`). `kind reaction`: `stance` = the player's dominant stance it answers, blank = fallback (one fallback with blank stance and blank president is required); the engine takes the stance match (president-specific first), else the fallback; a tie between the top two stances is `mixed` and reads the fallback. `$` beats, `[emotion]` tags and `_stage directions_` work as in dialogue. A lead without a sheet is a `TOWNHALL_SHEET` error and the moderator reads a stand-in.
- **Log.** `townhall` lines: `op start` (order, leads, president), `rating` (target speaker, block, value 1..7), `answer` (target topic, choice, stance code, text), `summary` (counts per stance, dominant), `end`. These, with the per-topic stance codes, are the town hall's measure; the log viewer renders them.
- **Checker.** `townhall.yaml` schema (`SCHEMA`, `BAD_VALUE`, `DUPLICATE_ID`; `TOPIC_STANCES` warning when a topic lacks one of the four codes), lead sheets as above, placeholders and `PACK_LEAK` on all town hall text (`{speaker}` is valid in `moderator.introduce` only).

## Profile questions and variant words (added 2026-10-19)

- **The opening questions.** The character form no longer asks the home culture. When a new game opens, the phone opens by itself on a single pane (`ui.profile.title`, no tabs, no close button) and nothing else can be done: exits are disabled, the scene is dimmed and not clickable (`.stage.profile-pending`), day 1 has not begun (`dayStartDone` stays behind, so the day-start events and the mother's message fire only after the answers, for the right pack). The questions come from `content/profile.yaml`: `heritage` ("What is the heritage culture that you most identify with before arriving in the US? If you were born in the US, what is the heritage culture that your parents most identify with?", options `cn | mx | in | other`) and, per pack, `followups` (a list of `{ id, question, options: [{ id, label, skip? }] }`); for `in` today: `region` (north | south | skip) and `faith` (hindu | muslim | skip). Choosing a heritage shows that pack's follow-ups; Begin is enabled when every shown question has an answer. On Begin: `player.heritage` = the option id, `player.homeCulture` = that pack (`cn` for `other`, with the note `ui.profile.otherCulture`), `player.pack` = the effective pack, `player.variant` = the follow-up answers minus skips (`{ region: 'south', faith: 'muslim' }`), `profilePending` cleared, one `profile` log line (`heritage`, `homeCulture`, `pack`, `variant`), then `start()` runs and the day begins. Eventually the answers come from the room-initiation survey; the phone pane is the stand-in and must stay a single screen. Dev: `?dev=1&culture=in` skips the questions (`&region=south&faith=muslim` set the variant); `&profile=1` asks them.
- **Variant words.** `strings.en.json` keys `pack.<pack>.<name>` (the base word, as before) and `pack.<pack>|<dim>=<value>[|<dim>=<value>].<name>` (a variant that applies when every listed dimension matches the player's `variant`), resolved by `packVars()` in `content.ts`: the most specific matching key wins, a skipped dimension falls back to the base. Defined today for every pack with Indian variants: `{homeLanguage}`, `{homeFestival}`, `{homeDish}`, `{homeSweet}`, `{homeWorship}`, `{homeRestaurant}`, `{homeSpecialty}`, `{homeRegion}` (values in strings.en.json). Writers template only what refers to the player's own background (what their mother cooks, the festival their family keeps); an NPC's own identity (Mrs. Nair's Onam, Harjit's gurdwara) stays. Grammar must survive every value of a placeholder. Lines a word swap cannot fix are listed in `docs/indian_variants_review.md` for a later row-level condition.
- **Checker.** `profile.yaml` is validated (`SCHEMA`, `BAD_VALUE`, `DUPLICATE_ID`); every `|dim=value` in a strings key must be a follow-up question and a non-skip option of that pack (`BAD_VALUE`); a variant word without a base for its pack is a `PACK_GAP` warning, as is a base missing for a pack; `UNKNOWN_VAR` knows every variant name. PACK_LEAK treats a `pack.in|...` string as Indian-pack text.

## Fuzz walker (added 2026-10-19)

`tools/fuzz/walk.mjs` (`npm run fuzz -- --seeds N --steps M`, see `tools/fuzz/README.md`) is a seeded random player over the running dev server in headless Chrome: at each step it lists what a player could click (choices, figures, hotspots, exits, the phone, module windows' buttons and radios; never dev-only buttons), picks one at random with weights that favour leading on in a conversation, and checks invariants at every step: no exception or `console.error`, no unfilled `{placeholder}` on screen, actions never below zero, no dialogue without an enabled choice, never stuck with nothing to click. It writes one transcript per seed from the game's own log and a compact `summary.md` with per-seed rows, row and event coverage, and failures grouped by message with `seed@step` for a repro (`--seed N`). It complements the checker: the checker proves rows reachable on paper, the walker finds what only happens at runtime. Output is git-ignored (`tools/fuzz/out/`).

## Conversation end, exits and the dev Back button (added 2026-10-18, night)

- **Conversation end.** Taking a leave option (a choice with no `next`) or the Continue button of a row without choices ends the conversation (`endConvo('ended')`), and the panel becomes idle with `ended: { npc, emotion }` (the last beat's emotion): the bust stays on the portrait area and fades back (`.portrait.ended .bust` and its chip animate to 30% opacity and mostly grey over 1.1 s; `prefers-reduced-motion` jumps to the end state) instead of the panel snapping to the scene description. The note under it reads `You part ways with {name}.` (`ui.convoEnded`). Anything that renders a different panel clears it.
- **Exits are locked while a conversation is on screen.** The exit strip's buttons (and the out-of-actions `Go home` button) are disabled with the title `Finish the conversation first` (`ui.inConversation`, class `exit locked`) while `panel.kind === 'dialogue'`, and `moveTo()` refuses with a console note; every row has a leave option, so the player is never stuck. `gv.goto()` passes `force: true` and still leaves (the conversation is then logged as `left`). Clicking another person or a hotspot still leaves the conversation as before.
- **Dev: stepping back.** Under the debug toggle (`?dev=1`), before every choice and every Next the engine keeps a JSON snapshot of the game state and the row/beat on screen in `convo.history` (the last 20); a dashed `◂ Back (dev)` button under the choices restores the previous one (state included: rapport, actions, wallet, the turn count and stay charge) so a writer can try another branch. The log is append-only, so the lines of the undone step stay and a `dev` line (`op: 'back'`, `target`, `rule`) marks the step back; the log viewer shows it inside the conversation. Not shown to participants.
- **Dev: editing a line in place.** Under the same debug toggle a dashed `✎ edit (dev)` button sits under the choices while a dialogue row is on screen. It turns the panel into an editor: a textarea with the row's whole raw `line` cell (as in the CSV: `$` beat marks, `[tags]`, `_stage directions_`, `{placeholders}`), one text input per existing choice text (effects, `next` and the number of choices are not editable here), and a one-line hint of the marks (`$` = split into a beat with Next · `[warm] [guarded] [worried] [neutral]` at a beat's start = that bust · `_words_` = stage direction). Save (or Cmd/Ctrl+Enter) writes the NPC's whole sheet into `localStorage['gv:override:v1'].dialogue[<npcId>]` with only that row's cells changed (starting from the stored override for that NPC if there is one, else from the bundled CSV as fetched, else regenerated from the loaded rows; same Papa.unparse quoting as the editor, header kept) and sets `updated`; Escape or Cancel drops the draft. An empty line or choice, or a sheet that no longer validates, is refused with an inline note, and saving is refused under `&nooverride=1`. The save hot-applies without a reload: the sheet is re-parsed, swapped into the loaded content, the panel shows the same row from its first beat (the conversation, its turn count and Back history go on), and the `edited content` chip appears. A `dev` log line (`op: 'edit'`, `target` = NPC, `rule` = row id) marks the edit. Not shown to participants.
- **Fade.** The bust fades to nothing at the end of a conversation (no grey: that read as an obituary). "Go to bed anyway" rings the phone for about 1.4 s before the check-in opens, so the call reads as a call. The phone sits above the "too tired" overlay.
- **One conversation at a time** (2026-10-19): while a dialogue is on screen the other figures and talk zones are disabled (`.locked`, aria "(Finish the conversation first)") and `talkTo` refuses a different person (`gv.talk` forces), the same way the exits are locked. The continue control between beats is drawn as a thin rule with a bobbing triangle in the middle (`.choice.next`), not as a choice button.

## Researcher editing, overrides and casting (added 2026-10-18)

- **Content overrides.** The engine loads bundled content, then applies an override set if one exists, so researchers can edit without a rebuild. Text lives in `localStorage['gv:override:v1']` as JSON: `{ "v":1, "updated":"<ISO>", "dialogue": { "<npcId>": "<csv text>" }, "news": "<csv>", "events": "<csv>", "npcs": "<yaml>", "scenes": "<yaml>" }` (each key optional; a present key replaces that whole file). Images live in IndexedDB database `gv`, object store `images`, keyed `"<section>/<id>/<variant>"`: `portraits/us_neighbor/neutral|warm|guarded|worried`, `figures/ch_us_neighbor_fig_stand`, `backgrounds/bg_home_day`, `tv/nat_inclusive/0`, `cast/<castId>/bust|tv`, `props/<propId>` (an events.csv `medium`, e.g. `props/prop_radio`); value `{ blob, type, w, h, updated }`. The engine resolves an image through the override store first (object URLs created at load) and the manifest second. A room may pin an override snapshot: `roomConfig.override = { text: <the JSON above>, imageKeys: [...] }` so participants on another machine see the researcher's edits once a server exists; for the demo, overrides are per browser. The game shows a small "edited content" chip in the top bar when an override is active; `?dev=1&nooverride=1` ignores it.
- **Editor** at `/editor.html` (Vite entry, `src/editor/**`): pick an NPC, see its sheet as a table (conditions, priority, emotion, line, three choices with effect and next), edit cells inline, add a row, add a follow-up (creates a row with priority -1 and met yes and links it from a choice), duplicate a row, delete a row (refused if something `next`s to it). Live validation uses the checker's core (`tools/checker/core.mjs`, pure functions shared with `check.mjs`): schema errors, missing fallback, dangling next, ties, coverage, and per-row reachability summary. Also edits `events.csv` and `news.csv` as tables, and swaps images (portrait per expression, figure, background, TV still, cast, event `medium` prop) by file upload with a preview at game size. Save writes the override; Export downloads the changed files as CSV/YAML/PNG so they can be committed into `public/content` and `image_generation/out`. Revert per file.
- **Cast (authority figures).** `news.csv` gains an optional `figure` column and `events.csv` an optional `portrait` column, both naming a cast id. Cast assets come from `image_generation/out/cast_<id>_ref_A.png`, `cast_<id>_bust_A.png` (1:1, transparent) and `cast_<id>_tv_A.png` (16:9 news framing); the asset build writes `manifest.cast[<id>] = { name, role, bust, tv, ref }`. The TV module shows the story's `figure` still when set (else the national leader's); print news shows the bust beside the headline; an event with `portrait` shows that bust on the right panel. The national leaders remain `nat_enforcement` / `nat_inclusive` with their own stills.
- **Choice order.** Choices are shown in a deterministic shuffled order per (room seed, row id, day) so the exit or the low-valence option is not always last. Log lines keep `choice` = authored index (1..3) and add `shown` = displayed position. The checker and editor use authored order.
- **Pay when you stay** (replaces "Short conversations are free", 2026-10-18 evening). Starting a conversation is free and turns 1-3 are free; the choice into turn 4 costs 1 action once; a `cost N` option costs N (max with the stay charge when both apply). Starting needs only that the scene is usable: tired() (0 actions away from home) still forbids it, as it forbids every interaction there, so in practice nobody starts a talk at 0 actions; a conversation already open can be finished at 0 actions on its free turns. The checker mirrors this in its cost model (`optionCharge` in `core.mjs`, with the turn in the search key).
- **Debug toggle.** `localStorage['gv:settings']` JSON `{ "debug": true|false }`, set from a single switch on the researcher page ("Show debugging aids"). Off hides: the Show interactables button and the H reveal, the footer rule/lean/phase line in the right panel, valence and rapport in Contacts, the dev guides, and the `rule …` chip in text panels. `?dev=1` forces it on for that tab. Rooms created from the researcher page default to off; `DEV1` defaults to on.

## Presence, place and conversation limits (added 2026-10-18)

- **Where people stand.** Every slot in `scenes.yaml` must be a spot where a person can plausibly stand or sit in that background: open floor, not on furniture, not intersecting a table, with a short comment naming the spot. Seated slots sit on actual seating. Slots are reviewed against gridded renders of each background (`tools/scenes/grid.mjs` draws a 10% grid and the horizon over each background into `tools/scenes/grid/`), and each scene records its `horizon` and `cameraHeight` as before.
- **Nobody is at home at wake-up.** NPCs have no appearances in `home`. Visits are events (`trigger: enter`, `scene: home`, with `npc`) that show the bust on the right panel and read as someone at the door or on the phone.
- **Per-appearance figure.** An appearance may name a `figure` asset to use in that scene (`{ scene: library, slot: by_shelves, chance: 0.3, figure: ch_us_stranger_a_fig_stand }`); blank = the NPC's default. A seated figure (which includes its stool) is only valid on a slot marked `seated: true`; the checker enforces this.
- **Dialogue location.** The dialogue CSV gains an optional column `scene` after `dayPhase`: blank = anywhere; otherwise one scene id or several separated by `|`. Selection first keeps rows whose `scene` is blank or contains the current scene, then applies the existing priority and specificity rule (a non-blank `scene` adds 1 to specificity). Completeness is checked per scene the NPC can appear in (from `appearances`, plus any event `scene` naming the NPC): for every such scene, every one of the 432 cells must be served, with the fallback row (all conditions blank, `scene` blank) still mandatory. Rows whose `scene` names a scene the NPC never appears in are an error.
- **Conversation limit.** `roomConfig.maxTalksPerNpcPerDay` (integer, default 1; the researcher page exposes it; `?dev=1` keeps 1 unless `&talks=N`). Once an NPC has had that many conversations today, their figure and talk zone stay visible but show "Talked already today" (muted label, disabled) and do nothing. Follow-ups via `next` inside one conversation do not count. The checker's cost model assumes at most `maxTalksPerNpcPerDay` conversations per NPC per day over the 5-day demo (default 1, so at most five conversations per NPC in a run) when searching for witnesses.
- **How many people at once.** At most `maxPresent` cut-out figures stand in a scene at once: the scene's own `maxPresent` in `scenes.yaml` (positive integer), else `roomConfig.maxPresent`, else 3 (`DEFAULT_MAX_PRESENT` in `src/rules.ts`; `?dev=1&present=N` overrides it for the tab). Painted NPCs (`painted`) do not count. Presence candidates are still resolved order-free (each appearance rolls on its own hash; lower roll wins, ties by NPC id) and are accepted in that (roll, id) order until the cap is reached; a candidate is also skipped when its figure would clash with an already accepted one. Clash, on the figure boxes exactly as drawn (slot x/y, horizon-locked `figureHeight`, the asset's aspect from the manifest or override, 0.45 when unknown): the nearer figure (larger slot y; equal y checks both ways) covers any part of the further figure's head band (its top 25%), or more than 40% of its box. So every figure on screen keeps its head visible and stays clickable. Dev placements (`gv.place()`) bypass both the cap and the clash check. The checker notes `SLOT_OCCLUSION` (info, not a warning) (scenes.yaml, naming both slots and the covered head-band and box fractions) for every slot pair that would clash under the same geometry, mirrored in `core.mjs`, taking per slot the tallest and widest figure that appears on it, and only when two different NPCs who can share a roster (same pack, or one shared) appear on the two slots. Slots are candidates, not simultaneous people, so there is no warning about how many slots a scene has. It is informational because the engine simply never shows both people at once; move a slot only when the two should be able to appear together (as of 2026-10-18: clinic near/by_cooler, clinic near/by_table, park path/path_far are left as mutually exclusive).
- **Figures appear with the scene.** The engine preloads the figure images for the present NPCs (and the background) before swapping the scene in, so people do not pop in a few frames late; a 150 ms fade covers the swap.
- **TV screens show the news.** A scene may list `screens: [{ id: tv, quad: [[x,y],[x,y],[x,y],[x,y]] }]` with the four corners of the TV glass in fractions of the background (top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left, measured on the gridded render). The engine draws the current TV story's still (the cast still or the leader still that `tvContent()` would show) onto that quad using a CSS `matrix3d` homography from the unit square, dimmed to ~55% brightness, slightly blurred (1.5 px at 900 px wide), with a faint vertical gradient so it reads as a lit screen seen from the room. It sits under the hotspot so the click still opens the TV. When the TV has been watched this phase the screen can show the second story; when there is no story, it stays dark.

## Character profiles, TV once a day, conversation limit semantics, conversation graph (added 2026-10-18, later)

- **Profiles.** Each NPC in `npcs.yaml` may carry a `profile` block, writer-facing and never shown to participants: `integrationLean` (0 = keeps the heritage world separate, 10 = fully blended; the roster's `stance` stays the research manipulation), `warmth` (0–10), `talkative` (0–10), `backstory` (3–5 sentences), `motivation` (one sentence: what they want from the player), `voice` (how they talk: sentence length, habits, what they never say), `topics` (list), `avoid` (list). The engine ignores it; the editor shows it as a Character card beside the sheet and the checker validates ranges. Mrs. Chen carries the example.
- **TV motion** (2026-10-19): the still on the room screens drifts and zooms slowly inside the glass (`tv-drift`, 16 s) with a faint flicker, so the set reads as switched on; off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. With `FEATURES.tvKeepWatching` off the TV module receives one story and shows no "Story 1 of 2" counter.
- **TV and newspaper are free** (2026-10-19). Watching the TV and reading a newspaper cost no actions, whatever `cost` the data gives (`hotspotCost` in `src/game.ts`): their hotspots show no cost dots, stay clickable at 0 actions wherever the scene is usable (at home always; tired() still closes every interaction away from home at 0 actions), and their `window`/`hotspot` log lines carry no `delta.actions`. Keep watching (feature off) spends 0 and shows no dots. The once-a-day TV rule below is unchanged.
- **TV once a day.** The TV can be opened once per day (`state.tvWatchedDay`). The projected screen shows the day's still until the TV window has been closed, then goes black for the rest of the day. The "Keep watching" second story is hidden behind `FEATURES.tvKeepWatching = false` (kept in code). After watching, the hotspot stays but is disabled with no notice; its aria-label says "TV (watched today)".
- **Conversation limit semantics.** The per-NPC daily count increments when a conversation ends (choice with no `next`, or leaving), never at its start and never mid-tree; a conversation in progress can always be finished, including after a reload. When the count has reached the limit the figure or talk zone is simply non-interactive: no hover outline, default cursor, no label; aria-label "<name> (talked today)" for assistive tech only.
- **Conversation graph.** The editor's dialogue tab offers a Graph view: nodes are rows (id, first words, emotion colour, scene tag, entry rows outlined), edges are choices (labelled with the choice number and short text) to their `next`; layered left-to-right by depth with entry rows in the first column; clicking a node selects the row in the table and highlights predecessors (upstream, amber) and successors (downstream, teal), so a writer can read a conversation backwards as well as forwards. Rows with no incoming edge and not winning any cell are flagged. An option with a `cost N` shows `●N` on its edge label (and "costs N actions" in its tooltip); the tree view marks the row shown on turn 4 "pay to stay +1" and a `cost N` edge "(cost N)". Pan with drag, zoom with wheel, fit button. No external libraries. Focus view (added 2026-10-18): clicking a node (or picking a row while a focus is open) spreads that row out in the middle of the graph over a dimmed overview: an expanded card with its full line (one block per `$` beat with its emotion, `_stage directions_` in italics on their own line, `{placeholders}` marked), emotion, scene, conditions and priority; to its left the rows that lead here, each with the full text of the choice that leads here; to its right one card per choice with its full player-facing text, its effect and its `●N` cost, pointing at a card for the row it leads to (or "ends the conversation", or a missing `next` in red). The view pans and zooms (briefly animated) so the focused card is centred and the cluster fits where it can. Clicking a choice or any row card moves the focus there, so a writer walks a tree by clicking; ↑/↓ move between the choices, → follows the choice, ← goes to a row that leads here, Enter follows; Escape or a click on empty space returns to the overview at its previous pan and zoom. The focus redraws live as the row is edited in the table.

## Culture packs, stances and the assimilation leaders (added 2026-10-18, evening)

- **Stances.** `stance` on an NPC is one of `none | integration | assimilation | separation` (Berry's acculturation strategies minus marginalization). Local leaders come in all three stances on each side: a host-community leader who wants newcomers to become indistinguishable (`assimilation`), one who wants a shared civic life that keeps both cultures (`integration`), one who wants the communities kept apart (`separation`); and the mirror on the heritage side (an assimilationist who urges dropping heritage markers, an integrationist, a separatist). Ids: `<pack>_leader_asm | _int | _sep` and `us_leader_asm | _int | _sep`. The checker validates the enum.
- **Packs.** A scene may carry `pack: cn | mx | in`; the street hub and exits list only shared scenes and the scenes of the player's pack (`center_cn` is `pack: cn`; `center_mx` and `center_in` are new scenes on `bg_center_mx_day` / `bg_center_in_day`). The player's effective pack is their `homeCulture` when `npcs.yaml` has at least one NPC in that pack and a scene carries it; otherwise `cn` with the existing banner. `{homeCulture}` text substitution follows the effective pack (Chinese, Mexican, Indian).
- **Rosters.** Each pack has six people: neighbor, stranger_a, stranger_b, leader_asm, leader_int, leader_sep, with `community: home` except one bicultural stranger_b. The shared roster gains `us_leader_asm`. Every NPC needs the eight required row types; leaders' rapport 2+ rows under matching playerLean `invite` an event. Dialogue for the mx and in packs follows the same quiet-cue rule: concrete, ordinary, no stereotypes (a parish hall, a cricket match on a phone, a tiffin, a quinceañera budget, a notary who does everyone's taxes).
- **Pack rooms.** The living room is culture-neutral by default; `home` carries `byPack` variants (image_generation 194-199, gentle edits of `bg_home_tv_day/eve_A` that change only three small objects each and leave the TV, window and door untouched: cn a blue-and-white bowl of mandarins, a lidded tea cup, a red knot on the bookshelf; mx a Talavera-style bowl, a striped woven blanket on the sofa arm, a barro negro vase; in an unlit brass diya, a block-print cushion cover, a tulsi plant) and the bookshelf `look` has `textByPack` naming them. Until the images exist every pack sees the neutral room. See "Content schemas" for `byPack`/`textByPack`.
- **Art.** Each new character: reference sheet, neutral/warm/guarded busts, standing figure, in the existing request-file format (part 10). The asset build derives character sources from `npcs.yaml` (`portrait` and `figure` fields) instead of a hard-coded table, so a new pack needs no build change.
- **Pack-gated events and news; per-pack words** (added 2026-10-18, night). Shared content must not carry one pack's culture: an Indian-pack run once got a voice message about zongzi and a news alert about Mandarin interpreters. Three tools, in order of preference. (1) Write it neutrally when it is really shared (a family "who arrived in March", "flyers in other languages"). (2) A per-pack word in shared text is a placeholder filled from `strings.en.json` keys `pack.<pack>.<name>`: `{homeCenter}` (the Grand Avenue center / the San José parish hall / the India Association hall), `{homeLeader}` (the pack's integration leader), `{chessRival}` (Walt's chess regular), `{centerDrink}`; a writer adds a name by adding all three keys. This is the only way to vary a line in a shared dialogue sheet (dialogue has no pack column). (3) `events.csv` and `news.csv` take an optional `pack` column (cn | mx | in; blank = every pack). Variants share an id stem: the base id keeps its id (and is usually the cn variant) and the others add `_mx`, `_in` (`ev_daystart_mom_message`, `ev_daystart_mom_message_mx`, `ev_daystart_mom_message_in`). The engine shows a gated row only to its pack, and a blank-pack row to everyone except a pack that has a variant in the same id group (the id without a trailing `_cn|_mx|_in`), which replaces it (`rowInPack` in `src/content.ts`, used by event rolls and news picks). Variants are separate ids, so `fired` and `seenNews` need no change; invitations name one id, so invited events are written neutrally rather than as variants. A row tied to a pack's npc or scene is already gated and needs no `pack`. The checker's `PACK_LEAK` lint keeps this honest against `public/content/pack_terms.yaml` (see "Checker").

## Action log (engine)

Append-only JSON lines kept in `localStorage` under `gv:log:<room>`, plus `POST /api/log` attempted with `keepalive` and silently ignored on failure (server comes later). One line per action:
`{"t":"<ISO>","room":"7K3F","day":2,"actions":5,"type":"dialogue|choice|event|hotspot|move|window|survey|journal|day_end|conversation","scene":"library","target":"us_neighbor","rule":"A01","choice":1,"delta":{"us_neighbor.rapport":[0,1]},"lean":"balanced"}`

Conversation line: `{"type":"conversation","target":"us_neighbor","scene":"library","turns":5,"ms":48210,"rules":["DN02","DN09","DN20"],"exit":"ended|left|day_end","cost":1}` (`cost` = actions the conversation's choices spent: stay charge, `cost N` and `actions -N`). The `dialogue` line that opens a conversation has `"cost":0` (starting is free); a `choice` line that was charged carries `"charged":N` (the stay charge or the option's `cost N`, whichever was taken; `"stay":true` when it was the turn-4 charge) and `delta.actions`. Survey line: `{"type":"survey","target":"<questionnaire id>","source":"server|room|bundled","answers":{...}}` where `answers` is the module's payload unchanged (BIIS-2 adds `scores`, `labels`, `instrument`).
Survey answers go in a `survey` line with `answers:{itemId:value}`. Journal saves go in a `journal` line with the full text. The researcher page exports the log as a `.jsonl` download.

## Window host API (engine builds modules too)

Modules are static HTML under `public/modules/` loaded in an iframe. ## Content schemas (content agent)

All files under `public/content/`. Text may use `{homeCulture}` (display name, e.g. "Chinese"), `{playerName}`, `{leaderName}`, and any `{name}` that `strings.en.json` defines for every pack as `pack.<pack>.<name>` (`{homeCenter}`, `{homeLeader}`, `{chessRival}`, `{centerDrink}`; see "Pack-gated events and news"); the engine substitutes.

`scenes.yaml`
```yaml
scenes:
  - id: home                 # also used as scene key everywhere
    name: Home
    community: neutral       # home | us | neutral  (drives playerLean)
    background: bg_home_day  # asset id; engine swaps to bg_home_eve in the evening if that asset exists
    lighting: warm_indoor    # warm_indoor | cool_indoor | outdoor
    byPack:                  # optional per-pack background variants (cn | mx | in), see below
      cn: { background: bg_home_cn_day, evening: bg_home_cn_eve }
    description: "A quiet morning. The TV is still on from last night."
    exits:
      - { to: street, label: "Front door" }
    hotspots:
      - id: tv
        label: "TV"
        zone: { x: 0.06, y: 0.10, w: 0.16, h: 0.18 }     # fractions of the background image, top-left origin
        action: { type: window, module: tv }               # free (TV and newspaper cost no actions)
      - id: newspaper
        label: "Newspaper"
        zone: { x: 0.45, y: 0.58, w: 0.12, h: 0.06 }
        action: { type: news }                            # free; engine shows a print story from news.csv in the right panel
      - id: bed
        label: "Bed"
        zone: { x: 0.86, y: 0.40, w: 0.12, h: 0.30 }
        action: { type: end_day }
    horizon: 0.33              # eye-level line (fraction of image height), measured from the background
    cameraHeight: 1.6          # optional, metres (default 1.6)
    slots:                     # where NPC figures stand: feet anchor as fractions (y may go to 1.3: feet below the frame, legs cropped)
      - { id: by_door, x: 0.80, y: 0.90 }          # height from the horizon formula below
      - { id: odd_spot, x: 0.50, y: 0.70, scale: 0.40 }   # optional explicit figure height / image height, overrides the formula
```
Action types: `window` (module tv|work|survey), `news`, `end_day`, `move` (to scene), `look` (shows `text` in the right panel, free), `talk` (`npc: <id>`; a click zone standing for a person painted into the background, free to start like any conversation, rendered only while that NPC is present through `painted`). A hotspot may carry `placeholder: true`: the engine then draws an always-visible dashed box with the word PLACEHOLDER over the zone, for art that is not in the background yet (the living-room TV until the Round 2 background lands).

Per-pack variants (added 2026-10-18). `byPack: { <cn|mx|in>: { background: <asset id>, evening?: <asset id> } }` on a scene names a background that replaces `background` for players of that pack (`player.pack`), and its evening render (default: the variant id with `_day` → `_eve`). Resolution in `bgFor`: an active `painted` variant wins; otherwise the pack variant is used when its asset is real (an editor override, or a manifest entry that is not a placeholder), else the scene's `background`. In the evening the pack's evening render is used when real; if the pack has a day variant but no evening one, the base's `_eve` render is drawn (right light, neutral objects). The engine remembers which base background a variant stands in for (`bg_home_cn_day` → `bg_home_day`, `bg_home_cn_eve` → `bg_home_eve`), and `screens`/`props` `byBackground` resolve as `byBackground[drawn] ?? byBackground[base] ?? quad`, so a pack edit that keeps the TV where it was needs no new quads. Variants preload with the scene like any background. A `look` action may carry `textByPack: { <cn|mx|in>: text }`; the player's pack text is shown, `text` stays the fallback (and is still required). Checker: bad pack keys and empty texts are errors; a `byPack` asset not in the manifest yet is an info `PENDING_ASSET` (as events' `medium`), not a warning, because the base background is drawn meanwhile. The asset build lists the variants in `BACKGROUNDS`/`BG_SOURCES` with no fallback source, so a missing one is only the usual "missing background" build note.

Figure size is horizon-locked: height / image height = (slot.y − horizon) × personHeight / cameraHeight, with personHeight 1.70 m standing and 1.30 m seated (the seated assets include the stool or bench), clamped to 0.12..0.95. On an eye-level shot this puts a standing adult's head just above the horizon at every depth. In dev mode the H reveal also draws the horizon and each figure's head line.

Staging convention: every scene has a `near` slot (feet at or just below the bottom edge, no scale) for the person the player is clearly there to talk to; incidental or overheard people take the smaller slots further back, or are painted into the background. A scene may declare `painted: { background: <asset id>, npcs: [<id>, ...] }`: when that asset exists in the manifest and every listed NPC is in the roster, the engine shows that background instead, skips the scene's composited props, treats the listed NPCs as present without drawing a figure, and reaches them through the scene's `talk` hotspots. Otherwise the plain background and cut-out figures are used, so content never waits on art.

Placeholders: wherever art is missing (background, figure, bust, TV still, placeholder hotspot) the engine shows the word PLACEHOLDER in small amber capitals on a dark chip, plus a grey silhouette for people. `street` is a hub scene with no background art: the engine renders it as a simple map card listing exits; give it `background: none`.

Scenes for the demo and their backgrounds: `home` (bg_home_day/eve), `work` (bg_work_day), `clinic` (bg_clinic_day, community us), `library` (bg_library_day, us), `park` (bg_park_day, neutral, the integrated space), `center_cn` (bg_center_cn_day, home), `street` (hub). Composite the TV prop (`prop_tv`) into home and clinic as a `prop` entry: `props: [{ asset: prop_tv, x: 0.06, y: 0.10, w: 0.16 }]`; the park's chess game is baked into its background (request 193). Look at the actual images in `image_generation/out/` to place zones and slots on real furniture.

Props (`props: [{ asset, x, y, w, quad?, byBackground? }]`): by default a prop is drawn flat, its top-left at `x, y` and `w` wide (fractions of the background), under figures and hotspots. A flat prop image can be projected onto a surface seen in perspective, with the same homography as TV screens: `quad: [[x,y],[x,y],[x,y],[x,y]]` gives where the corners of the whole prop image land (top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left, fractions of the background, measured on the gridded render); the engine lays the image out at its manifest `w`x`h` and maps it onto the quad with a CSS `matrix3d` (transform-origin 0 0), slightly softened, and `x/y/w` remain the flat fallback. `byBackground: { <background asset id>: quad }` overrides the quad on one of the scene's backgrounds (its `background`, `painted.background`, or the evening variant of either; the checker rejects other ids). The chessboard in the plain park (`bg_park_day`, Mexican and Indian packs) was first done this way and looked pasted on, so it is now baked into the photo instead (image request 193, `bg_park_board_day_A`, an edit of `bg_park_day_A` with a game in progress on the nearest table; the build prefers it once it exists) and the park has no props; the quad remains available for flat props such as papers on a desk. The painted chess variant skips props as before. In dev mode `gv.propQuad(<scene>, <asset>, [[x,y],[x,y],[x,y],[x,y]])` sets a quad in memory (never saved), `null` draws the prop flat, and omitting the quad drops the override; it sits next to `gv.screens('home', quad)`, which does the same for TV screens.

Writing dialogue: pace (added 2026-10-18). People in this game talk the way people do, not in aphorisms. On a first meeting the opening row is a plain greeting that says who the person is in ordinary words ("Good morning. Ernesto Villaseñor. I do the tax returns here on Saturdays. You are new in the neighbourhood?"), with a normal reply or two and a polite exit; no backstory, no thesis about identity. The second turn is small talk or the practical thing at hand (the pantry, the form drawer, the dripping shower). Only from the third turn on does information arrive, and then as an aside hooked to what the player just asked, one thread per turn that the player may follow or not: the notario past comes up because the player asked whether he is an accountant, not because he announced it. The identity and culture material, which is the research content, belongs there, on the fourth or fifth turn, and depth reached is itself a measure. Revisits skip the introduction, open with a greeting and one ordinary thing, and pick up a thread at the same unhurried pace; rapport changes how warm the person is and what they volunteer, not how dense the lines are. Every turn offers a way to leave (a choice with no `next`). Lines are one or two plain sentences, sometimes three: hesitation, a question back, a concrete detail, about the length of a line in a quiet film. Keep the profile's `voice` and `avoid` lists. Cost: the engine charges by staying, not by starting. Starting and the first three turns are free, and the choice that leads on to the fourth turn costs one action (once per conversation; the player sees the amber dot on that choice before taking it), so the introduction and small talk cost nothing and the player pays exactly when the conversation turns to the material that matters. Do not pad or cut a tree to dodge the charge; let a turn-4 thread be worth an action. A substantive ask, where the person wants the player's time ("could you help me carry the boxes to the pantry?"), goes on the option that accepts it as an explicit `cost 1` or `cost 2` in its effects; it is charged instead of the stay charge when both fall on the same choice, and the refusal next to it stays free. `mx_stranger_a`, `mx_neighbor` and `us_neighbor` are written this way and serve as models; on 2026-10-18 (night) the other 23 sheets were rewritten to the same rule (roughly 45 to 70 rows each).

Writing dialogue: beats and stage directions (added 2026-10-18, night). A `$` inside a `line` (dialogue) or `text` (events) splits it into beats shown one at a time, the visual-novel way: the panel shows the first beat with a single continue button (a bobbing ▾ triangle, the visual-novel cue; accessible name "Next"), then the next, and the row's choices (or the event's) only with the last beat. A beat may open with `[neutral]`, `[warm]`, `[guarded]` or `[worried]` to change the bust for that beat; otherwise the row's `emotion` column applies to every beat, and the bust that stays on the panel when the conversation ends is the last beat's. Beats are not turns: pressing Next does not advance the conversation's turn count, so the stay charge ("Pay when you stay") is unaffected; the log gets a `beat` line (`target`, `rule`, `beat` = the 1-based beat now shown, `event: true` for an event). A short stage direction standing between sentences in underscores or asterisks (`Here a notary is a man with a stamp. _scowls_ And the flyers count on people not knowing that.`) is shown on its own line in italics (`.line.direction`), the way a script prints an action; it is text only and does not change the bust (set the emotion column or a beat tag for that). The opening mark must follow a space or the start of the beat and the closing mark must be followed by space, punctuation or the end, so `snake_case` ids and `2*3` are left alone; one stage direction per beat is plenty. Checker: `$` is validated (`checkBeats` in `core.mjs`): an empty beat (two `$` in a row, or a trailing one) and an unknown `[tag]` are errors (`BAD_VALUE`), more than three beats in one line is a warning (`BEATS`). Reach and cost analysis ignore beats, as the engine does. Writers use beats where a pause helps (a reveal, a change of mood), a handful per sheet, two beats per line as the norm and three the most.

## Checker (checker agent)

`node tools/checker/check.mjs [--content public/content] [--report tools/checker/report]` → exit 1 on errors. Loads scenes, npcs, dialogue, events with js-yaml and papaparse. Reports:
1. Schema errors (unknown columns, bad condition values, unknown effect tokens, `next` pointing nowhere, missing fallback row, unknown scene/slot/asset references between files).
2. Completeness per NPC: enumerate the cell space (met 2 × rapport 4 × lastValence 3 × nationalLeader 2 × playerLean 3 × dayPhase 3 = 432) and run the exact selection rule; list ties (error), cells served only by the fallback (coverage %), rows that never win (warning).
3. Reachability per NPC: build the transition model from the data: start state per NPC from `initial`, world variables free to move (over-approximation), edges = dialogue choices' effects (costed as in "Pay when you stay": the edge into turn 4 costs 1 once per conversation, a `cost N` option N, max of the two when both apply, plus `actions -N`) and events' effects involving that NPC, each costing its action cost; BFS for minimum action cost per cell; flag rows whose every matching cell costs more than 40 actions (5 days × 8) or is unreachable; emit one witness path per reachable row (sequence of row ids / choices).
4. HTML report: `report/index.html` with a per-NPC coverage grid (rows = rapport × met, columns = lastValence × nationalLeader × playerLean × dayPhase, cells coloured by specificity of the winning row, hover shows the row id) and the lists above. Dependency-free HTML.
5. Culture packs (added 2026-10-18, night; `tools/checker/packs.mjs`). `pack` on events/news must be cn | mx | in or blank (`BAD_VALUE`); a pack that contradicts the row's npc or scene pack is `PACK_MISMATCH` (nobody sees it). Completeness: if any row of an id group (id minus a trailing `_cn|_mx|_in`) is pack-gated, every pack needs a variant or the group a blank-pack fallback, else warning `PACK_GAP` (groups tied to one pack's npc or scene are exempt); likewise a `pack.<pack>.<name>` string must exist for all three packs. `UNKNOWN_VAR` (warning): a `{name}` in events, news, dialogue or scenes that the engine cannot fill. `PACK_LEAK` (warning): every player-facing text is scanned for the per-pack keyword lists in `public/content/pack_terms.yaml` (`cn`, `mx`, `in`; 15-30 lowercase terms each, whole words or phrases, case-insensitive, optional plural s; `{placeholders}` skipped). Text every pack sees (events/news rows with a blank pack and no pack npc or scene, shared dialogue sheets us_* and nat_*, shared scenes' names, descriptions, hotspot labels and look texts, exits to shared scenes, strings except `culture.*`, the questionnaire's title, intro, items and options) must use no pack's terms; text one pack sees (gated rows, that pack's NPC sheets and scenes, `textByPack.<pack>`, `pack.<pack>.*` strings) must use no other pack's terms. Fix the content; a term that turns out to be legitimately shared comes off the list with a comment saying why (the file explains why `grand avenue` alone, `ortiz` and `tea` are absent); a single deliberate cross-pack mention goes under `allow:` in pack_terms.yaml with a `why` (an unused entry is reported). The CLI reads pack_terms.yaml, strings.en.json and questionnaire/eod.json; the editor's in-browser run does not pass them, so these lints run from `npm run check`.
Also `tools/checker/README.md`. Include 3 small fixture sheets under `tools/checker/fixtures/` that exercise a tie, a shadowed row and an unreachable row, and a `node tools/checker/check.mjs --content tools/checker/fixtures/bad` run that shows them.
